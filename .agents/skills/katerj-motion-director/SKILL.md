# KaterJ Motion Director

## Outcome

Turn an explanation into a visible sequence of state changes so the result behaves like a video rather than a deck with transitions.

## Workflow

1. State the moving thesis: object, starting state, ending state, and claim proved by the transformation.
2. Build a continuous beat graph with time, narrative job, moving object, state change, text role, asset need, and slideshow risk.
3. Choose two to four recurring motion primitives such as reveal, wipe, route, branch, transfer, compression, comparison, or settle.
4. Keep the page or scene base stable; animate meaningful components in narration order.
5. Give still images narrative movement through masks, crops, parallax layers, or state transitions.
6. Bind hero terms and reveals to final word timestamps rather than estimated scene midpoints.
7. Render a short sample when the visual language materially changes, then carry the approved grammar into `katerj-video-director`.

## Sponge host with synchronized typography

- Size the complete native illustration to about 85% of its source dimensions when a side-by-side explanation needs more room. Place the sponge's visual center near the vertical boundary between the middle and right thirds of the 16:9 frame. Do not crop a model sheet, enlarge beyond native pixels, or cover embedded handwritten labels.
- A group of related comparison cards uses one shared width, calculated from the longest rendered title/detail plus padding and a long-card aspect floor. Do not use separate arbitrary widths. Keep all cards visually equal; only the currently spoken/selected card receives emphasis or motion.
- Caption panels and standalone text backings follow measured text width. Reuse the caption renderer's sparse local backing-color emphasis for the selected phrase rather than painting a full-width band.
- For a static full-scene raster, use an outline-first, color-second draw-on of semantic regions. A small hand visibly holding a pencil tracks the drawing tip; the hand sprite must have real transparency, stay within the drawing area, and avoid hiding native words except momentarily at the active stroke. Reveal the character and scene base first, then reveal each native label or object detail across its corresponding spoken cue. Completed regions remain visible. Bind formal reveal boundaries to verified word timing; sample timing is only a rhythm prototype.

## Anti-slideshow gate

Reject a plan built from repeated title-and-bullet pages, identical card layouts, whole-page drifting, decorative motion with no state change, or long reading blocks. Most beats must visibly transform, travel, reveal, compare, or resolve.

## Acceptance

- Every beat has a narrative job and visible state change.
- Adjacent scenes vary their component structure.
- Text supports the motion rather than carrying the whole explanation.
- Contact-sheet and semantic-cue frames prove continuity and timing.
